Hall I

The Girl with Blue Hair

Coraline Jones is bored, brave, curious and very hard to fool. This room is about her.

I.1

Her name started as a typo

Neil Gaiman meant to type "Caroline". His fingers typed "Coraline" instead, and he decided it looked like a real name that nobody had used yet.

I.2

From Michigan to Oregon

In the film she is eleven and has just moved with her parents from Pontiac, Michigan, to the Pink Palace Apartments in Ashland, Oregon. She misses her friends and has nothing to do.

I.3

The blue hair came from the film

The book never gives her blue hair. The filmmakers gave her the bright blue bob, and now it is the first thing anyone pictures.

I.4

The dragonfly clip

She keeps her bangs back with a dragonfly hair clip. Add a yellow raincoat and rain boots and you have the outfit every Coraline costume starts with.

I.5

An explorer from the first scene

The film opens with her walking the overgrown grounds with a forked stick, dowsing for an old well. She finds it, and nearly falls in.

I.6

Her voice

Dakota Fanning voices Coraline in the film.

She's brave, curious and a little stubborn. Remind you of anyone? xo, J

Hall II

The Pink Palace

An old pink house split into apartments, full of odd neighbours. Here is the residents' register.

  • Mom · voiced by Teri Hatcher

    Mel Jones

    Always busy and usually on deadline. She and Charlie write a gardening catalogue together, and neither of them actually gardens.

  • Dad · voiced by John Hodgman

    Charlie Jones

    The family cook. Coraline is not a fan of what he makes.

  • Neighbour · voiced by Robert Bailey Jr.

    Wybie Lovat

    Short for Wyborne. He was created for the film so Coraline would have someone to talk to. He does not exist in the book.

  • No address · voiced by Keith David

    The Cat

    A black cat with no name. He never says a word in the real world, but in the Other World he talks, and he knows far more than he lets on.

  • Ground floor · Jennifer Saunders & Dawn French

    Miss Spink & Miss Forcible

    Two retired actresses with a house full of Scottish terriers. They read Coraline's tea leaves and warn her she is in danger.

  • Top floor · voiced by Ian McShane

    Mr. Bobinsky

    A retired acrobat who is training a circus of jumping mice. He can never get her name right and keeps calling her Caroline.

  • Landlady

    Mrs. Lovat

    Wybie's grandmother owns the Pink Palace. When she was a girl, her twin sister disappeared in that house.

  • Behind the little door · also Teri Hatcher

    The Other Mother

    Voiced by the same actress as Coraline's real mom, which is exactly why she feels so familiar at first.

Our apartment would have way fewer stuffed dogs. Maybe one. xo, J

The Beldam

The ghost children call the Other Mother "the beldam", an old word for a witch or a hag. Her true form is thin and spider-like, with needles for fingers.

The ghost children

Three children who said yes to the button eyes long ago. Coraline promises to find their eyes and set them free.

The seeing stone

Miss Spink and Miss Forcible give Coraline a stone with a hole in it. Looking through it helps her find what the Other Mother has hidden.

No world is perfect. Ours is still my favorite. xo, J

Hall IV

The Workshop

The 2009 film is stop motion. Every movement you see is a real puppet, moved by hand a tiny bit at a time and photographed one frame at a time.

IV.1

Knitted with needles as thin as a hair

Coraline's tiny sweaters were knitted by the artist Althea Crome, using needles about as thin as a human hair.

IV.2

Printed faces

Her faces were made on 3D printers, in two halves: one for the eyes and brows, one for the mouth. Swapping the halves gave her those 207,000+ expressions. The line where they met was erased digitally, frame by frame.

IV.3

LAIKA's first film

Coraline was the first feature from the studio LAIKA. It was shot on sets built in a huge warehouse in Hillsboro, Oregon, near Portland.

IV.4

The director

Henry Selick directed it. He also directed The Nightmare Before Christmas and James and the Giant Peach.

IV.5

The music

The score is by Bruno Coulais, with a children's choir singing words in a made-up language. The song the Other Father sings is by They Might Be Giants.

IV.6

Oscar night

It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. It lost to Up.

They moved tiny puppets one frame at a time for years. That's how carefully I want to love you. xo, J

Hall V

The Library

Before the movie, Coraline was a book. This shelf follows her from the first page to the big screen, twice.

  1. 1990s

    The story begins at bedtime. Neil Gaiman starts writing it for his daughter Holly. It takes him about ten years, and he finishes it for his younger daughter, Maddy.

  2. 2002

    The book comes out, with spooky ink illustrations by Dave McKean.

  3. 2003

    The awards arrive. It wins the Hugo Award for Best Novella, then the Nebula Award and the Bram Stoker Award too.

  4. 2008

    A graphic novel version, adapted and drawn by P. Craig Russell.

  5. 2009

    The film opens in the US on February 6. That same year, an off-Broadway musical opens in New York with songs by Stephin Merritt.

  6. 2010

    Oscar nomination for Best Animated Feature.

  7. 2024

    Back in theaters in 3D for its 15th anniversary, and it becomes a hit all over again.

Book vs. film

No Wybie

In the book, Coraline faces the Other Mother completely alone.

No blue hair

The blue bob, the dragonfly clip and the yellow raincoat look all come from the film.

The hand in the well

In the book, Coraline tricks the Other Mother's hand into the old well by herself. In the film, Wybie helps her.

I've lost count of how many times we've watched it. Let's keep losing count. xo, J

my love,

The Other Mother offered Coraline everything: the best food, the brightest garden and a whole world built around her. In return, all she asked for was Coraline's eyes.

Coraline said no. She went home to the rain, the mud and the dinners she didn't like, because the real world was the one with the people she actually loved.

That's how I feel about you. I don't need a perfect world. I need this one, because you're in it.
